# 1. Introduction

Social engineering is a form of cyberattack that relies on manipulating individuals rather than exploiting technical vulnerabilities. Attackers use deception, persuasion, and psychological manipulation to trick victims into revealing sensitive information, granting unauthorized access, or performing actions that compromise security.

Social engineering attacks remain one of the most successful methods used by cybercriminals because they target human behavior rather than computer systems. This report examines three common forms of social engineering attacks: phishing, pretexting, and baiting.

---

# 2. Phishing Attacks

## Definition

Phishing is a cyberattack in which attackers impersonate legitimate organizations or individuals to deceive victims into revealing sensitive information such as usernames, passwords, credit card details, or banking information.

## How Phishing Works

Attackers typically send fraudulent emails, messages, or create fake websites that appear trustworthy. Victims are encouraged to click malicious links, download infected attachments, or provide confidential information.

Common phishing methods include:

* Email phishing
* SMS phishing (smishing)
* Voice phishing (vishing)
* Spear phishing

## Impact on Organizations

Phishing attacks can result in:

* Data breaches
* Financial losses
* Identity theft
* Unauthorized system access
* Reputational damage

---

# 3. Pretexting Attacks

## Definition

Pretexting occurs when an attacker creates a fabricated scenario or identity to gain the trust of a victim and obtain confidential information.

## How Pretexting Works

The attacker pretends to be someone with authority or a legitimate reason for requesting information. Examples include impersonating:

* IT support staff
* Bank representatives
* Company executives
* Government officials

The victim may unknowingly provide passwords, account information, or sensitive organizational data.

## Impact on Organizations

Pretexting attacks may lead to:

* Information disclosure
* Unauthorized access
* Financial fraud
* Compromised business operations

---

# 4. Baiting Attacks

## Definition

Baiting is a social engineering attack that offers something enticing to persuade victims into taking an action that compromises security.

## How Baiting Works

Attackers may leave infected USB drives in public places or advertise free software downloads containing malware.

Examples include:

* Free movie downloads
* Free software offers
* Infected USB devices
* Fake giveaways

When victims interact with the bait, malware may be installed on their systems.

## Impact on Organizations

Baiting attacks can result in:

* Malware infections
* Data theft
* Network compromise
* System disruptions

# 5. Real-World Case Studies

## Google and Facebook Phishing Scam

Between 2013 and 2015, a cybercriminal impersonated a legitimate supplier and successfully tricked employees at Google and Facebook into transferring more than $100 million through fraudulent invoices.

### Impact

* Significant financial losses
* Demonstrated the effectiveness of social engineering
* Highlighted weaknesses in verification procedures

---

## Twitter Social Engineering Attack (2020)

Attackers targeted Twitter employees through social engineering techniques and gained access to internal systems.

### Impact

* High-profile accounts were compromised
* Cryptocurrency scams were promoted
* Damage to platform reputation

---

## USB Drop Attacks

Security researchers have repeatedly demonstrated that employees often plug unknown USB devices into company computers.

### Impact

* Malware installation
* Unauthorized network access
* Data compromise

# 6. Prevention and Mitigation

Organizations can reduce the risk of social engineering attacks by implementing the following measures:

## Security Awareness Training

Employees should receive regular cybersecurity awareness training to recognize suspicious emails, messages, and requests.

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds an additional layer of security, making it more difficult for attackers to gain access using stolen credentials.

## Verification Procedures

Organizations should establish procedures to verify requests involving sensitive information, financial transactions, or system access.

## Email Security Solutions

Email filtering systems can help detect and block phishing attempts before they reach users.

## Restricting Removable Media

Organizations should control the use of USB devices and scan removable media before use.

---

# 7. Conclusion

Social engineering attacks continue to pose a significant threat to organizations worldwide. Unlike technical attacks that exploit software vulnerabilities, social engineering attacks exploit human trust and behavior.

Phishing, pretexting, and baiting remain among the most common social engineering techniques used by cybercriminals. Through employee training, strong authentication mechanisms, and effective security policies, organizations can significantly reduce their vulnerability to these attacks.

A combination of technical controls and user awareness is essential for defending against modern social engineering threats.
